# Intro

Whisker Scope shows what your sound is doing: its waveform, its frequencies, how wide it is, and how close it is to a sound you want to match. Everything you see is measured from a copy of the audio. The sound goes through untouched unless you use Input gain, turn on the matching EQ on the Shapes page, use Output gain, or press Panic.

It is free and open source, made by Studio 87. It collects nothing and works fully offline.

# Quick start

- Put Whisker Scope on the track or the master you want to look at. Play something.
- Pick a page at the top: Scope, Spectrum, Stereo field or Shapes.
- Hover the Spectrum to read the frequency, the note and the level under the mouse.
- Input gain changes the level going into the plugin: the sound, the displays and the meters all follow it. SC gain only changes what you see of the sidechain.
- Hover any control for one second to read what it does. Settings can turn those bubbles off.

# Scope

The waveform of the sound. Channels picks L / R, left, right, mono or mid / side. Sync keeps the picture steady: Free shows the newest samples, Trigger starts each sweep at a rising zero crossing, Pitch locks to the detected pitch and shows two periods, Beat shows one beat of the host tempo. Visible time sets how many milliseconds you see. Freeze holds the picture.

# Spectrum

Which frequencies are in the sound. FFT size trades sharpness (bigger, good for bass) against speed (smaller). Speed sets how fast the picture falls. Range sets how many dB are shown. Smoothing averages neighbouring frequencies. Slope tilts the picture by dB per octave around 1 kHz (3 dB makes pink noise look flat). Linear switches to a linear frequency scale, Fill colours under the line, Peak hold keeps the highest value of every frequency until Peak reset. The highest peak is labelled with its level, frequency and note.

## Key readout

The box in the top right corner of the Spectrum guesses the key of what you play: the key name, the Camelot code (8A is A minor, 8B is C major) and a confidence value. It listens to about 6 seconds of the sound between 100 Hz and 2 kHz and compares the notes it hears with typical major and minor profiles (Krumhansl-Kessler). It needs a sustained tonal sound: chords, pads, a melody over some bars. It shows "Listening" at first and "No clear key" for noise, drums only or sweeps. A single chord is ambiguous with its relative key (C major and A minor share the same notes), so the box then shows "or" and the second guess; it is a hint, not a verdict. Key hold freezes the readout so it stays while you play something else. Key reset forgets what it heard and starts again.

# Stereo field

The goniometer shows where the sound sits between left and right. Straight up is mono, sideways is wide, a diagonal means one side only. The correlation meter goes from -1 (antiphase, cancels in mono) through 0 (stereo) to +1 (mono). Width is the share of side energy. Mono loss is how much quieter the sound gets when left and right are added. The verdict (Mono safe, Check mono, Phase problem) follows simple rules of thumb: it is a hint, not a standard.

# Shapes

Shapes compares the average spectrum of a reference (a song, a lead you love) with your own sound, on 60 bands of 1/6 octave from 20 Hz, Mid and Side kept apart.
- Press Capture reference, play the reference, press the button again. Only moments with signal are averaged.
- Press Capture mine and do the same with your sound. Without it, the live spectrum is compared.
- The bars at the bottom are the EQ that moves your sound toward the reference (pink cuts, teal boosts). Both shapes are first levelled to their own average, so loudness does not matter, only the balance of frequencies.
- Strength scales the curve. Max move limits the largest boost or cut.
- EQ on turns the matching Mid/Side EQ on in the audio path, so you can hear it. It is 30 bells per channel and every control is automatable. Auto level keeps your loudness or matches the loudness stored with the reference. Input and Output set the level around the EQ. A safety limiter sits at the very end.
- Load file analyses an audio file on your computer and uses its average Mid and Side shape as the reference, with a progress bar and a Cancel button. It runs only on your computer and nothing is uploaded. WAV, AIFF, FLAC and OGG always work; MP3 and others only where your system can decode them (macOS and Windows usually can, Linux builds do not read MP3). Files shorter than 2 seconds or longer than 60 minutes are refused, and silent parts are skipped.
- Save as preset keeps the current reference (captured or from a file) under a name in your library, in the Studio 87 / Whisker Scope folder on this computer. The drop-down "Or pick a reference preset" lists the starting points and your own. Manage renames, deletes, exports (as text) and imports presets. Bad text is refused.
- The starting points are made by formula and are not measured from any song. Since v0.3.1 they include genre presets. "(study)" ones follow a published study of chart records (medium confidence); "(rule of thumb)" ones are our own constructions from producer advice and "(rough guess)" has no published data behind it, so treat those as a first idea only. Sources and the numbers are in docs/genre-curves.md in the repository. A real reference song of your own is always better than a preset.
- Copy EQ points copies the bells (frequency, gain, Q) for Mid and Side, to type into any other EQ.

# Sidechain layer (masking)

Route a second track into the plugin's sidechain input in your host (layer B). It is drawn in yellow on the Spectrum, Scope and Stereo field and is never added to the output. On the Spectrum, Masking marks in red the frequency ranges where your track and the sidechain track are within the chosen distance (3, 6 or 10 dB) of each other, which is where they may mask each other. SC gain only changes what you see.

# Window

Drag any edge or corner to resize; the shape stays the same (25 % to 250 %). Double-click a wooden side for the default size. The size is remembered.

# Levels

On the right: level bars for left and right (IN and OUT on the Shapes page) with peak and RMS readouts, crest factor, a held peak (Peak reset clears it) and the CLIP light. Metering and RMS length choose how they react. Below them are correlation, width, balance, mono loss and the Mono safe verdict. These are monitors, not certified meters.

# Recommendations

- Match shapes with the same kind of material: a lead against a lead, a full mix against a full mix.
- Treat the EQ curve as a hint. Move a little, then listen.
- Use Mono safe as a quick check before you export, and listen in mono once.
- Capture for a few seconds of the busiest part of the song, not the intro.
